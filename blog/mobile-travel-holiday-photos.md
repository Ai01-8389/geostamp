# How GeoStamp Saved My Holiday Photos From Becoming 'Some Beach, 2025'

Two years ago I went to Japan. I took 847 photos across 12 days. Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka, a day trip to Nara, a random stop at a temple whose name I couldn't pronounce.

Today I can tell you maybe 30% of where those photos were taken. The ones I shared on Instagram still have the location pinned. The rest — the 600+ photos sitting in a folder — are just "some temple," "some street," "some restaurant."

I swore next trip would be different. So last month, visiting Thailand, I tried something new.

## The Trip: 9 Days in Thailand

I brought only my phone — an Android Galaxy S23. No DSLR, no action camera. Just the phone in my pocket, the same way most people travel.

### Day 1: Chiang Mai — The Problem Appears

First morning, I visited three temples, a local market, and a cooking class. By lunchtime I had 62 photos. I already couldn't remember which temple was which.

**Old me:** Would have taken the photos, gone home, and six months later asked "was this Wat Phra Singh or Wat Chedi Luang?"

**New me:** After the cooking class, while waiting for my pad thai, I opened Chrome on my phone, went to `geostamp.top`, and uploaded the batch.

GeoStamp read the GPS coordinates from the EXIF data (still intact because I hadn't shared any photos yet) and stamped each one with the location name and coordinates.

The temples got their names. The market got coordinates. The cooking class got a timestamp alongside the address.

### Day 4: Phi Phi Island — GPS Is Useless Without a Stamped Photo

The ferry dropped us at Tonsai Pier. I took photos of the beach, the cliffs, the longtail boats. Same turquoise water, same white sand — 30 photos that would later blur into one memory.

I opened GeoStamp on my phone right there at the beach (4G signal was fine) and stamped the batch. The coordinates on those photos now read `7.7407° N, 98.7785° E` — that exact stretch of beach, not "some island."

### Day 7: Floating Market — The Real Test

The Damnoen Saduak floating market was chaotic. I took photos of boats, food, souvenirs, and people. By now I'd fallen into the habit: every evening before dinner, I'd open GeoStamp on my phone, upload the day's batch, download the stamped versions.

10 minutes per day. That's it.

## Three Weeks Later, Back Home

I have 534 stamped photos organized by date and location. I can tell you:

- Which boat noodle stand we ate at (coordinates on the photo)
- Which temple we visited on the morning of Day 3 vs the afternoon of Day 4
- Exactly where we watched the sunset on Day 6
- The GPS stamp even helps me remember what we were doing — because "where" triggers "when" triggers "what happened"

## Why Phone Photos Lose Location

Your phone is the best travel camera you own. But its location data lives in invisible EXIF metadata. The moment you:
- Share a photo via messaging app (stripped)
- Upload to social media (stripped)
- Back up to cloud storage (often stripped)
- Send via email (often stripped)

...the location is gone, and you're left with "some beach, somewhere."

## How a Web App on Mobile Fixes This

GeoStamp runs in your phone's browser. No app store, no account, no installation. The process for any traveler:

1. **Take photos** with your phone camera (GPS auto-records)
2. **Before you share**, open `geostamp.top` on your phone
3. **Upload the batch** — the EXIF data is still there
4. **Download stamped versions** — location burned into the photo
5. **Now share anything** — the location travels with the image

## What I Wish I Knew Before Japan

The difference between "I traveled somewhere" and "I remember where I was" is one stamp per photo. And for a traveler with only a phone, spending 90 seconds per day on a browser tool is the easiest insurance against "some beach, 2025."
