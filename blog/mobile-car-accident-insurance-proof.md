# A Fender Bender on My Way to Work — Until My Phone Photos Proved Where It Happened

Last month I rear-ended someone at a traffic light. Minor damage, nobody hurt. The other driver and I pulled over, exchanged information, and I thought that was it.

Two weeks later, his insurance company claimed the accident happened at a different intersection. They said their client was stopped at a red light on Oak Street — I was supposedly at the intersection of Oak and 5th.

I was certain it was Oak and 3rd. My dashcam had run out of SD card space the day before (of course). It was my word against his.

## The Save: Phone Photos With Location Stamps

I had taken five photos at the scene with my phone — both cars, the damage, the intersection. Standard "document the accident" photos.

I hadn't shared them yet (no WhatsApp, no text). So the EXIF data was still intact.

I opened `geostamp.top` on my phone right there in the parking lot where I'd pulled over. Uploaded the five photos. GeoStamp read the GPS coordinates and stamped them onto each image.

The result: four photos showing the intersection of **Oak and 3rd**, with GPS coordinates, date, and timestamp burned directly into the image.

I emailed the stamped photos to my insurance adjuster. The other company dropped their claim the next day.

## Why This Works for Accident Photos

Phone cameras automatically record GPS coordinates into EXIF data. Most people don't realize this. And most people don't realize it gets erased the moment they text the photos to someone.

The trick is **stamping the location before you share**.

### The Simple Flow

| Step | What Happens | Time |
|------|-------------|------|
| 1 | Take photos with phone at the scene | 30 seconds |
| 2 | Open GeoStamp in mobile browser before leaving | 45 seconds |
| 3 | Upload + download stamped versions | 30 seconds |
| 4 | Send stamped photos to insurance | 15 seconds |

**Total: under 2 minutes, from bumper to stamped proof.**

## What the Insurance Adjuster Told Me

I called my adjuster afterward and asked what mattered most. He said:

> "Most people send us blurry phone photos with no location data. We have to take their word for where it happened. A photo with a visible GPS stamp? That's evidence. We don't argue with coordinates."

## Keep Your Phone Camera Ready

Your phone is the best accident documentation tool you have. It's always with you. It records GPS automatically. The only missing piece is making that location visible before you hit "send."

GeoStamp on mobile takes 90 seconds and has saved me a drawn-out insurance dispute. I now have a browser bookmark on my phone's home screen — `geostamp.top` — and I use it every time I document anything that might need proving later.
